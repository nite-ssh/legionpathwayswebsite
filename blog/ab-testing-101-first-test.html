A/B Testing 101: How to Run Your First Test Without Wasting Traffic

By Nitesh Poudel · 7 min read

Most first A/B tests fail for the same two reasons: they test something too small to matter, or they get called too early. Both are avoidable once you know the basic shape of a valid test.

Pick a hypothesis, not just a change

"Let's try a different button color" isn't a hypothesis. "Shoppers aren't clicking add-to-cart because the price feels uncertain without visible shipping cost" is — and it points to a specific, testable change with a reason attached. If you can't articulate why a change should help, it's a guess, not a test.

Test high-traffic pages first

A test on a page with low traffic can take months to reach a reliable result. Start with your highest-traffic landing page, product page, or the checkout flow itself — not because they're most important, but because they'll give you a usable answer fastest.

Decide your sample size and stopping point before you start

The single most common mistake is checking results daily and stopping the moment one version pulls ahead. Early leads reverse constantly. Set your minimum sample size and test duration before launch, and don't call the result until you hit it.

A null result is still a result

If a test shows no meaningful difference, that's valuable information — it tells you that particular lever isn't where your conversion problem lives, and you can move your attention elsewhere with more confidence.

Key takeaways

  • Start from a specific hypothesis, not a random idea to try.
  • Test your highest-traffic pages first to get a readable result faster.
  • Set your sample size and duration before launch, and don't stop early.
