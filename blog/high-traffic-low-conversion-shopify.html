Funnel diagnostics

You Have Traffic. You Don't Have Conversions. Here's Where to Look First.

By Nitesh Poudel · 7 min read
Illustration: Funnel diagram: traffic in, revenue out

"We have plenty of traffic, it's just not converting" is one of the most common things a founder says right before spending more money on ads to fix a problem ads can't fix. Traffic gets people to your site. What happens after that is a completely separate problem, with its own set of causes — and its own set of fixes that don't cost a dollar in ad spend.

Traffic and revenue are two different systems

It helps to think of your funnel as a series of separate handoffs, each with its own conversion rate: ad to landing page, landing page to product page, product page to cart, cart to checkout, checkout to completed order. A traffic problem shows up as low volume at the very top. A conversion problem shows up as a steep drop at one specific handoff further down — and it's easy to confuse the two if you're only looking at the final number.

The four places drop-off usually hides

1. The landing page doesn't match the ad

If someone clicks an ad promising 30% off and lands on a generic homepage with no sign of that offer, they bounce immediately — not because the offer wasn't compelling, but because the page broke the promise that got them there. Landing page mismatch is one of the highest-drop-off, easiest-to-fix problems in the entire funnel.

2. The product page doesn't answer the objection that's actually stopping the sale

Missing size guides, no visible return policy, thin or absent reviews, unclear shipping timelines — each of these leaves a specific doubt unanswered at the exact moment someone is deciding whether to trust you with their money. Session recordings and on-page surveys are usually the fastest way to find out which doubt is actually costing you the sale on a given product.

3. The cart creates friction before checkout even starts

Confusing quantity controls, no visible way to apply a discount code, or shipping costs that only appear after clicking through to checkout all create hesitation before someone even reaches the point of entering payment details.

4. Checkout itself asks for too much

This is the stage covered in detail in our checkout optimization checklist — and it's usually the stage with the steepest, most consistent drop-off of any point in the funnel.

How to find out which one is actually yours

You don't need to guess. A short diagnostic process usually surfaces the answer within a week:

  • Pull your funnel analytics stage by stage — landing, product, cart, checkout, purchase — and find the single steepest drop, not just the overall conversion rate.
  • Watch session recordings at that specific stage. Ten to fifteen sessions usually reveals a pattern much faster than staring at a dashboard.
  • Check heatmaps on the page where the drop happens to see whether people are engaging with the content that's supposed to move them forward, or ignoring it entirely.
  • Prioritize by volume, not intuition — a small percentage improvement at your highest-traffic drop-off point usually beats a large improvement somewhere few people ever reach.

Key takeaways

  • Traffic and conversion are separate problems with separate causes — more ad spend doesn't fix a broken product page or a confusing checkout.
  • Drop-off tends to concentrate at four points: landing page mismatch, unanswered product-page objections, cart friction, and checkout friction.
  • Session recordings and heatmaps, read alongside stage-by-stage funnel data, usually identify the real cause faster than assumptions do.
  • Fix the highest-traffic drop-off point first — that's where a small improvement returns the most revenue.

If you've been increasing ad spend to compensate for a conversion problem, the funnel is usually where that money is actually going.
